# V10.1 diagnosis — what I found when I took over the game

Written before any code was changed. Every finding below was traced to specific lines by one of fifteen investigators (first launch, the core loop, endgames, the rival, saves, settings, the AI features, the play screen, performance, determinism, the world systems, content, robustness, the test setup, and a real playthrough in a browser). A second reviewer then tried to *refute* every serious finding from the code; **3 of 173 were refuted and dropped, and about half were downgraded** (the skeptic's corrected severity is what is ranked here). Line numbers refer to the file as it stood at the start of this pass (`(V9.9) australia-game-with-ai.tsx`, 272,209 lines).

## Where the game stands

* **The file is not V9.9.** It already carries V10.0 (sections 20AY and 20AZ, label `V10.0`, rules stamp 1000), so this release is **V10.1** and its sections start at **20BA** (after 20AZ, before Section 21).
* **Baseline (recorded before touching anything):** `tsc --strict` 0 errors (40 s). Self-tests in a real browser: **2,629 run, 2,628 pass**; the one failure is `v92 gl_perf`, a timing check that depends on machine load (22–31 ms against its budget). Release check: 135 of 136. A headless Node runner I added (`tools/selftest/node-run.mjs`) reproduces those numbers exactly in about a minute, which is what made fast iteration possible.
* **It is a big, careful, honest codebase.** The previous releases left a trail of explanatory section headers, a rules stamp that lets balance change without breaking old saves, and 70 test suites. The problems below are not sloppiness; they are what happens when 270,000 lines grow by feature layers and nobody plays the result end to end.

## The headline: the game is currently *solved*, *unsafe*, and *unfair in both directions*

A player who only ever presses the Recommended Action's "Do It" button wins a 29-day match from $1,000 to **$132,856, grade S, with no bad day** (real browser playthrough). Separately, buying at the Resource Market and reselling in the same region is a **riskless, compounding money engine** (median $34k from $1,000 without ever moving, against $16k for the best of the game's own scripted strategies). The rival, meanwhile, plays by *different rules* from the player in ways that cut both ways (it pays an action to sell, the player does not; it travels at a flat $350 where the player pays up to $800 × weather × season). The player who loses a match to a closed tab loses it silently, because both safety nets are off by default and nothing tells them they exist.

## Ranked findings (by damage to the player's experience)

Severity is the verified one (5 = blocks play or loses progress for most players; 4 = frequent, in a mainstream flow; 3 = real but occasional or mode-specific; 2 = polish). "Fix" means fixed in this pass; "Defer" means left for later, with the reason.

## What I am adding (all off by default)

1. **Rival Recap.** The rival's turn is the one part of the game the player cannot influence and has to wait through (7–12 s). Today it leaves five-second toasts. A recap card — what it did, why (from the real ranked candidates it chose between), what each move made or lost — turns waiting into reading, and gives the rival a character. Setting: `rivalRecapEnabled`.
2. **Text size (A− / A+).** The setting already exists (`textScaleMultiplier`) and nothing reads it, while ~200 literals on the play screen are under 12 px. Wiring it is the cheapest accessibility win in the file. 100 % by default.
3. **Daily Challenge with fixed rules.** The Daily Seed fixes only a seed, so "best today" compares different matches. Pinning character, length, win goal and rival makes the daily comparable and gives players a reason to come back. Setting: `dailyFixedRulesEnabled`.

Why these over the alternatives: a *rewind-the-turn* undo was the other strong candidate, but with a seeded RNG it becomes "learn the outcome of a risky challenge, rewind, don't take it", which needs a design (limited uses, an "assisted" mark) I'd rather not improvise. *Authored campaign chapters* are the biggest content gap (eight identical matches, a rival that is only a name) but are an L-sized feature, not a pass. The three chosen are small, self-contained, off by default, and each fixes something a player or the owner will feel.

## How this release is built (so you can trust it)

* One file, no new dependencies, no `Math.random` in game logic, no `any` in new code.
* **Rules changes ride the existing rules stamp.** A match started on V10.1 carries `gameState.rulesVersion = 1010`; a V10.0 match (1000) and older keep the rules they started with, in saves and replays. `classicRulesV100` (off) stamps a new match with the V10.0 rules, as `classicRivalV99` did for V10.0.
* New code lives in sections **20BA–20BK** (before Section 21); everything else is a marked in-place fix: `// V10.1 (SECTION 20Bx): …`.
* Self-tests: one suite per work package (`v101fx` … `v101en`), registered like V9.9's and V10.0's.

### Honest remaining gap (deferred on purpose, measured)

The ladder now slopes, but **Hard and Expert are still easier than I wanted for the Tourist**, and **the characters are not balanced**: at equal play the Tourist earns about 43% more than the Businessman, and the Scientist wins only about 26% at Medium. No rival-side lever can close this because the rival plays by the player's rules and cannot be given a hidden bonus. The measured fix is a V10.1-gated Tourist pay change (x1.2 to x1.1) plus a Scientist odds perk; on a hooked copy that puts the Tourist ladder almost entirely inside the target bands (balanced 98/80/66/73, casual 93/66/56/47) but it touches about six live sites and the Scientist stays at 27%, so I did not ship it half-done. Related balance notes: a player who only presses Recommended → Do It now ends around $75k (grade S) on Easy against $132k before; the live-economy lab ends a market-only line near $358 (below the $1,000 start) at default Game Length, which is deliberate but is an owner judgement.
